### Competition · how hard to be the lawyer

Civil plaintiff side · High competition for good cases

Named / visible CO firms and practices that already own this lane: Newman McNulty · Maxted Law · Civil Rights Litigation Group · Novo Legal · Highlands / other §1983 boutiques · ACLU co-counsel on select cases. Marketing is SEO-heavy on “police brutality Denver” and contingency intake. Big cases travel to firms with trial track records and war chests.

Criminal defense adjacency · Medium competition

Many CD firms mention civil rights lightly. Few Springs firms are known plaintiff-civil-rights brands. Pav can own “charged after a bad stop / excessive force in your arrest” without pretending to be Newman McNulty on day one.

Barrier stack for P2: contingency financing · federal practice · QI motion practice · municipal counsel depth · expert medical/use-of-force witnesses · 2-year ELEIA clock · reputation for taking losers hurts SEO trust.

### Probability of “winning”

No clean public plaintiff win-rate for Colorado excessive-force trials. Use this decision frame:

| Path | Realistic outcome pattern | Notes |
| ---- | ------------------------- | ----- |
| Internal affairs complaint | Low sustain rate on force | Denver OIM: community complaints often not sustained · use-of-force specs are a small share |
| Federal §1983 | Hard · qualified immunity kills many claims at summary judgment | 10th Cir. regularly grants QI · some reverse QI denials when force is obvious |
| Colorado ELEIA CRS 13-21-131 | Better pleading path · no QI defense | Still few filings · still need strong facts · fees to prevailing plaintiff |
| Settlement after bodycam | Common end-state for strong cases | Cities pay to avoid trial · not a jury “win” |
| Weak bruise / unclear video / resisting narrative | Often decline or lose early | Plaintiff firms already skim and reject |

Rule of thumb for intake: video + injury + charge dismissal or weak criminal case = higher civil viability. He-said vs officer with no video = usually decline for P2.
