## 1. Research snapshot


| Pattern               | Typical market practice                                                                           | Fit for GGL                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Cash**              | Deposit **scales down** with fee size (GGL: 50% → 30% → 20% → …) · balance on delivery/milestones | Default P1                       |
| **Milestone**         | 30/40/30 or deposit → mid → completion                                                            | Implementation projects          |
| **Retainer**          | Invoice **in advance**; unused monthly capacity expires; overage = fixed change-order add-on      | Paid media + ops stewardship     |
| **Deferred services** | Provider prices risk like credit — expect **≥~12%** return on deferred balance                    | Partial deferral “higher return” |
| **Late / overdue**    | Often **1.5% per month** on unpaid invoices                                                       | After missed written due date    |


Sources to cite if a client asks “why a premium?”: deferred arrangements behave like financing the client’s cash flow; risk premium is normal ([Eight One Partners — deferred payments](https://www.eightonepartners.com/blog/deferred-payments)); consulting projects use deposits/milestones ([Toggl consulting pricing models](https://toggl.com/blog/consulting-pricing-models)); retainers billed in advance ([NetSuite retainer guide](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/erp/consulting-contract-retainer.shtml)).

**Legal marketing performance note:** Do **not** default to bonuses based on signed cases, signed clients, legal fees, settlements, or average case value. For outside marketing vendors, that creates fee-splitting / pay-for-recommendation risk. Use fixed-dollar marketing/ops KPI bonuses (answer rate, booked-consult rate, qualified lead logging, CRM completeness, reporting milestones, campaign launch milestones) unless the law firm’s ethics counsel approves a signed-case-rate structure in writing.

## 2. Payment plan types


| Code   | Plan                             | When to offer                                 | Cash timing                                                     | Premium / return                            |
| ------ | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **P0** | Pay in full (cash)               | Strong cash, simple SOW                       | 100% before start or Net 15                                     | **1.00×** list                              |
| **P1** | Standard deposit (by fee size)   | Most projects                                 | Deposit % from **§8 ladder** · balance on delivery / milestones | **1.00×**                                   |
| **P2** | Partial deferral + higher return | Client wants cash relief; good credit / trust | Pay **cash share** now; defer rest                              | **Premium on deferred share only** (see §3) |
| **P5** | Monthly retainer                 | Ongoing stewardship                           | Invoice **month in advance**                                    | No deferral on retainer (cash)              |


**Stacking rules**

- **P2:** Use for audit/project cash relief with a written due date.  
- **P5:** Never defer retainer months.  
- **Media spend:** Always client → platforms; never mixed into deferred consulting balance.  
- **Cap deferred principal:** Suggest max **$15k–$25k** open deferred across one client unless Kate raises it.

## 3. Partial deferral

**Base idea:** Cash portion pays **list price**. Deferred portion pays **list × (1 + premium)** when it comes due. Premium compensates for time + collection risk.

### 3a. Premium by % deferred

Use the longest duration only: **up to 6 months**. Shorter terms can use the same premium or be discounted in the SOW.


| % of fee deferred | Max duration | Premium on deferred balance |
| ----------------- | ------------ | --------------------------- |
| **10%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +10%                        |
| **20%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +12%                        |
| **30%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +14%                        |
| **40%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +16%                        |
| **50%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +18%                        |
| **60%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +20%                        |
| **70%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +22%                        |
| **80%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +24%                        |
| **90%**           | Up to 6 mo   | +26%                        |
| **100%**          | Up to 6 mo   | +30%                        |


*Use 100% deferral only for a trusted client with a written due date and co-client liability.*

### 3b. Quick calculator

```
Cash due now       = List × (1 − D)
Deferred principal = List × D
Additional cash    = Deferred principal × Premium
Total client pays  = List + Additional cash
```

**Additional cash** = what you earn for allowing the deferral (not a %). Quote SOWs as **“X% premium on deferred balance due on [date]”** (= $ additional cash).

### 3c. Worked examples (list = **$1000**)


| Defer % | Duration   | Premium | Cash now | **Total paid** | **Additional cash** |
| ------- | ---------- | ------- | -------- | -------------- | ------------------- |
| 0%      | —          | —       | $1,000   | **$1,000**     | **$0**              |
| 10%     | Up to 6 mo | 10%     | $900     | **$1,010**     | **$10**             |
| 20%     | Up to 6 mo | 12%     | $800     | **$1,024**     | **$24**             |
| 30%     | Up to 6 mo | 14%     | $700     | **$1,042**     | **$42**             |
| 40%     | Up to 6 mo | 16%     | $600     | **$1,064**     | **$64**             |
| 50%     | Up to 6 mo | 18%     | $500     | **$1,090**     | **$90**             |
| 60%     | Up to 6 mo | 20%     | $400     | **$1,120**     | **$120**            |
| 70%     | Up to 6 mo | 22%     | $300     | **$1,154**     | **$154**            |

## 4. Savings-share rider for financial-management work

Use only when an SOW includes a financial audit, cost-reduction implementation, vendor renegotiation, or cash recovery. This rider is additional to the fixed project fee and its P0/P1/P2 payment plan.

**Default savings share:** **20% of verified net savings or recovered cash** attributable to Consultant’s documented recommendation or implementation.

| Change type | Measurement period | Savings-share treatment |
| ----------- | ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Subscription, SaaS seat, phone line, domain, hosting, directory, or other recurring-service cancellation / downgrade / consolidation | **12 months** from the effective change date | 20% of actual net reduction during the period |
| Vendor, telecom, insurance, processing, or other continuing contract-rate reduction | **6 months** from the effective change date | 20% of actual net reduction during the period |
| Advertising, LSA, staffing-schedule, or operating-process revision where spend varies with usage | **3 months** after implementation | 20% of verified reduction against the signed baseline, adjusted for volume |
| Refund, billing credit, disputed charge, duplicate payment, or other one-time recovery | Once, when posted or received | 20% of cash or credit actually recovered |

**Verification and billing**

1. Baseline = the pre-change recurring charge, contract rate, or trailing 90-day average documented in the signed savings register.
2. Net savings = baseline cost minus actual post-change cost, less direct implementation costs, early-termination charges, replacement costs, taxes, and third-party fees.
3. Recurring savings-share invoices issue **quarterly in arrears** from statements or vendor invoices. One-time recoveries invoice after the credit or cash posts.
4. No implementation or no realized savings = no savings-share fee. If a recurring change is reversed, savings stop on the reversal date.
5. Do not double-count overlapping changes or amounts already included in another fee.
6. Exclude reductions caused only by lower client volume, headcount cuts not recommended by Consultant, market-price changes, taxes, debt principal, legal fees, settlements, case value, trust funds, or Firm revenue.
7. The payment obligation survives project completion or termination only for each applicable measurement period above.

**Contract note:** Percentage savings compensation is based only on reduced operating cost or money actually recovered. It is never a percentage of legal fees, settlements, case outcomes, trust funds, or Firm revenue. Have counsel review the first signed rider.

## 8. Project deposit ladder (P1) — % falls as fee rises

**Rule:** Higher list fee → **lower** deposit %. Balance due on delivery, or split across mid + delivery when the fee is large.


| Project list fee (up to) | Deposit at start      | Remainder                                     | Sample on a max-band fee            |
| ------------------------ | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **$2,000**               | **50%**               | 50% on delivery                               | $2,000 → $1,000 + $1,000            |
| **$5,000**               | **30%**               | 70% on delivery                               | $5,000 → $1,500 + $3,500            |
| **$10,000**              | **20%**               | 40% mid · 40% delivery *(or 80% on delivery)* | $10,000 → $2,000 + $4,000 + $4,000  |
| **$25,000**              | **15%**               | 35% mid · 50% delivery                        | $25,000 → $3,750 + $8,750 + $12,500 |
| **$50,000**              | **10%**               | Custom milestones (written acceptance)        | $50,000 → $5,000 + rest per SOW     |
| **Above $50k**           | **10%** or negotiated | Custom                                        | Cap risk; counsel if needed         |


**How to read the band:** Use the row for the **smallest “up to” ceiling that is ≥ list fee**.  
Examples: $1,800 → 50% · $3,200 → 30% · $7,500 → 20% · $18,000 → 15%.

**If using P2 (partial deferral):** Cash share at start normally meets the §8 deposit. Kate may approve a higher deferred share, up to 100%, when the SOW has a written due date and co-client liability.


| Project list fee | Normal cash at start (§8) | Deferrable under P2       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| ≤ $2k            | 50%                       | 10–100% with SOW due date |
| ≤ $5k            | 30%                       | 10–100% with SOW due date |
| ≤ $10k           | 20%                       | 10–100% with SOW due date |
| ≤ $25k           | 15%                       | 10–100% with SOW due date |
| ≤ $50k           | 10%                       | 10–100% with SOW due date |


*Worked (≤$5k list $5,000): normal cash $1,500 (30%); deferred share and premium follow §3 and the SOW due date.*

## 11. Scaling rules

1. **Raise list first, then premium** — Don’t discount cash clients to match deferred; raise list 5–10% annually or per demand.
2. **Volume:** 3+ projects in one SOW → **5% list discount on cash (P0/P1) only** — never on deferred balance.
3. **Prepay retainer 3 months** → **5% off** those three months (cash).
4. **Risk score:** New client + P2 ≥50% deferred → require **co-client individual liability** (Firm + principal sign jointly/severally) or refuse.
5. **Equity / revenue share:** Out of default menu; separate counsel.
6. **Public site:** Keep only narrative (deferral + higher return + written due date). Numbers live here and in SOWs.
