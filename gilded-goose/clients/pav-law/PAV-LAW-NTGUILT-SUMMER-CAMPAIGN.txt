Executive Summary
NTGUILT is a focused summer growth campaign designed to turn Andrew’s 1969 Corvette, the NTGUILT vanity plate, and his natural connection to the Colorado car community into a practical business-development engine for Pav Law. The campaign gives the firm a memorable way to reach drivers, car enthusiasts, collectors, families, and referral sources in environments where traffic, DUI, DWAI, reckless driving, and license-related issues feel highly relevant.
The business value is twofold. First, the campaign can help build a steady traffic-defense pipeline that Casey and Jake can work over the next several months. These cases may not be the firm’s highest-value matters individually, but they create a reliable meat-and-potatoes income channel while we build a separate campaign for Andrew focused on higher-value criminal defense cases. Second, the campaign expands Pav Law’s visibility inside a referral-heavy community where people remember personalities, vehicles, stories, and repeat appearances. This approach will give us short term signed client boost while building long tail brand recognition.
The event mix is intentionally narrow, practical, and built around opportunities that are already in motion. PPIR provides the campaign’s core motorsports audience through repeated exposure to drivers, spectators, car clubs, racing families, and performance-car enthusiasts. Because two prepaid PPIR events have already passed, it is important to build as much campaign value as possible around the remaining schedule and turn the existing $20,000 marketing investment into measurable business results. These events are approaching quickly, so materials, lead-capture tools, raffle structure, follow-up workflows, and ad support need to be built now. 
Fan Fest and related Pikes Peak Hill Climb activity provide high-volume visibility with motorsports fans, local families, tourists, crews, and Colorado Springs-area drivers. Colorado Concours-style classic car shows add a more established collector audience with higher household-income potential, stronger local networks, and better long-term referral value. Fan Fest alone draws more than 35,000 people to Downtown Colorado Springs, Colorado Concours has historically drawn roughly 8,000–10,000 attendees and more than 300 vehicles, and PPIR events regularly attract thousands of repeat local spectators and drivers.

Why These Events? 
These events were strategically selected to deliver the highest concentration of qualified, private-pay prospects in the exact environments where DUI, traffic, and license issues naturally originate. The PPIR cluster (already paid) provides repeatable high-volume access with built-in booth space and passionate car-enthusiast traffic. The two Concours shows bring premium high-net-worth owners who can afford full-service representation. PPIHC volunteer registration plus Fan Fest create deep personal relationships and massive downtown visibility. The June-heavy schedule front-loads testing for immediate ROI proof, while the August Hot Rod and September Vail closers extend momentum and capture end-of-season leads. The result is a powerful, memorable summer-long DUI/traffic brand presence that turns Andrew’s Corvette and NTGUILT plate into a rolling lead-generation machine. We’d also like to layer in personal injury messaging towards the end of the season after getting the campaign up and running. 
Target Audience  
* Primary: Private-pay car enthusiasts, Corvette/classic/exotic owners, performance drivers, families with teen drivers, and high-net-worth collectors in the Colorado Springs / Pikes Peak / Denver metro region operate in environments where traffic stops, DUIs, reckless driving, and license issues are common.  
* Secondary: High-risk drivers who attend racing, off-road, or mountain events and are statistically more likely to need DUI/traffic defense.  
* Tertiary: Referral sources including club organizers, detail shops, tire dealers, and auto-insurance agents who interact daily with the same audience. Partnership and representation opportunities with other vendors. 


Marketing Plan
Booth Engagement  
Each booth is designed to feel comfortable, approachable, and easy to engage with so attendees stay longer, start real conversations, and move naturally into lead capture. Instead of setting up a passive vendor table, the booth should feel like a motorsports hospitality stop with seating, shade, cooling fans, cold drinks, popsicles, and interactive car-focused elements.
The Corvette should be parked as part of the booth display whenever possible so it serves as the main visual anchor, photo opportunity, and conversation starter. A small semi-private consultation area can be built around the car for quick intake conversations, with a playful sign such as Pit Stop Consultations, The Lincoln Lawyer, But Louder, or Legal Download Zone to make the setup feel more relaxed and event-appropriate.
Lead capture should be fast and low-friction. iPad-based forms and event-specific QR codes can be used for raffle entries, consultation requests, and active-case intake. Attendees should be able to complete the basic form in under 30 seconds while the team captures useful qualifying details on the spot. Racing headsets can help with clear conversations in a loud venue while also doubling as a photo-op prop.
Engagement should go beyond a breathalyzer test. The booth can include safety displays, camera demos, safe-ride cards, and giveaways that connect naturally to DUI, traffic, and driving safety conversations. This gives people multiple reasons to stop, even if they do not currently need legal help.
The booth should function as a hospitality-first motorsports activation rather than a standard vendor table. Comfortable race-inspired seating, fans, cold drinks, popsicles, and branded giveaways give attendees a reason to stop and stay. The Corvette serves as the main visual anchor, while racing headsets, jumpsuits, helmets, safety displays, and camera demos create natural conversation starters. Fast QR and iPad-based forms make raffle entry, consultation requests, and active-case intake easy to complete in under 30 seconds. Hourly swag raffles keep traffic active, while higher-value service giveaways and safe-ride offers create stronger follow-up opportunities after the event.

Employee Incentive Options
Marketing Campaign Support
Incentives reward event work, intake quality, speed, coverage, and overall campaign performance while creating a balanced program. Staff receive immediate rewards through a fixed daily event stipend, wellness and PTO support to prevent burnout, individual quarterly bonuses for personal contributions, and collective team celebrations when non-fee metrics or goals are met.
All options are structured as flat stipends, non-monetary perks, discretionary pools, or role-based recognition to remain fully compliant with Colorado’s Rules of Professional Conduct. No incentives are tied to a specific client signing, referral, or percentage of legal fees.
The amounts shown are rough estimates based on monthly revenue performance and can be adjusted to fit your actual usage and preferences.
* Combined Event Coverage Stipend & Reimbursement: $700 per full event day (flat stipend plus gas, meal, parking, and per-diem) for assigned weekend or evening coverage.  
* Extra PTO + Discretionary Wellness Recovery Days: 1 extra PTO day per weekend event worked, plus 1 discretionary wellness/recovery day after heavy months and a $500 wellness fund credit.  
* Campaign Performance Bonus Poll: Discretionary quarterly pool distributed individually based on role, hours, quality, and contribution.  
* Team Milestone & Celebration Budget: Combined flexible pool for group experiences or catered celebrations (spa day, dinner, outdoor outing, escape room, concert, etc.) when non-fee metrics or campaign targets are reached.  
* Event Gear, Fashion, Clothing & Presentation Allowance: Annual or per-campaign allowance for branded clothing, professional fashion, shoes, weather gear, and booth presentation materials.
